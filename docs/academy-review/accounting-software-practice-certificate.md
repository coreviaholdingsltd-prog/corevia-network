## 1. Scope and expected competence

This Certificate programme is an introductory, practical course. A learner should be able to:

1. Classify ordinary business transactions and explain the accounting equation, double-entry logic and chart of accounts.
2. Prepare customer invoices, allocate full and partial receipts, and distinguish bookkeeping records from the taxpayer's applicable KRA eTIMS workflow.
3. Capture supplier bills and expenses with traceable evidence, duplicate checks and approval controls.
4. Reconcile a cashbook to a bank statement, investigate differences and retain a correction trail.
5. Interpret basic profit-and-loss, balance-sheet and cash-flow information, including receivables and payables.
6. Apply basic user-access, backup, retention and month-end close controls.

All exercises and examples must use synthetic data. This course does not provide tax, audit or professional accounting advice and must not instruct learners to create live tax invoices or alter real accounting records without authority.

## 2. Subject-specific curriculum changes

The programme's six units have been given a course-specific curriculum in `lib/academy-subject-content.ts`:

| Unit | Subject focus | Required learner evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Accounting records, double entry and chart of accounts | Chart of accounts, transaction classification, journal examples and balanced trial-balance check |
| 2 | Customer records, invoices, receipts and eTIMS | Synthetic invoices, partial receipt allocation, receivables schedule and KRA source log |
| 3 | Supplier bills, expense evidence and payment controls | Document-control checklist, classification rationale, supplier ledger and approval trail |
| 4 | Cashbooks, bank feeds and reconciliation | Synthetic bank statement, cashbook, reconciliation statement and exception log |
| 5 | Financial reports, cash flow and management review | Profit-and-loss summary, balance-sheet snapshot, cash movement and receivables/payables ageing |
| 6 | Access security, backups and month-end close | Role-permission matrix, backup restore test, close checklist and unresolved-items register |

The learning tasks explicitly prohibit fabricated data, unsupported tax treatment, forced reconciliation adjustments, shared credentials and unauthorised changes to real books.

## 3. Assessment improvement

The Certificate pathway now uses six course-specific assessment questions instead of generic distractors. They test:

- owner contribution versus sales revenue;
- correct allocation of a partial customer receipt;
- supplier source evidence and approval;
- investigation of an unexplained reconciliation difference;
- revenue/receivables versus cash received; and
- month-end access, evidence and reconciliation controls.

The questions include explanatory feedback. A reviewer should still check that each item has one defensible best answer and aligns with the six units.

## 5. Suggested practical capstone

**Scenario:** A fictional Kenyan service business completes one month of operations.

Learner receives a synthetic opening balance, owner contribution, customer invoices, partial receipts, supplier bills, petty-cash expenses and a bank statement containing a charge and one unmatched item.

**Deliverables:**
1. Chart of accounts and transaction classification.
2. Sales invoice and receipts register with outstanding balances.
3. Supplier bill register with evidence and approval status.
4. Cashbook and bank reconciliation with an explained exception log.
5. Basic monthly profit-and-loss and cash movement summary.
6. Month-end close checklist, role/access matrix and reflection.

**Suggested rubric (100 marks):** subject knowledge 20; application and accuracy 25; evidence and reconciliation 25; controls, ethics and safety 15; communication and reflection 15. Pass threshold: 75/100, subject to the Academy's configured assessment rules.
